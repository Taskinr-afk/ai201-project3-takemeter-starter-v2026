## Milestone 1: Community and candidate distinctions

Community: r/claude

Four distinctions I noticed:

1. Showcases vs bug reports. A lot of posts are "look what I built" demos (games, renderers, full apps). The other big cluster is people asking if others hit the same problem (usage spikes, token drain, slow responses). One group shows output, the other crowdsources whether a bug is real.

2. Technical practitioners vs non-technical reactions. Some posts share workflows and habits from people building with Claude Code. Others are pure reaction with no specific technique, just awe or frustration at the experience.

3. Product complaints vs company complaints. "The new UI has too many clicks" targets the product. "Anthropic told the IMF something alarming" targets the company. Same negative energy, different object.

4. Reusable things vs one-off results. Some posts share a tool, workflow, or tip others can actually use. Others share a cool screenshot that invites upvotes but not imitation.

## Milestone 2: Labels

1. showcase — A post where someone shows off something they made with Claude. A game, a tool, a site, a visual, anything with a demo or result attached.
   Example 1: "Procedural generation with Opus is insane. This entire game is 20MB."
   Example 2: "I built an FFT ocean renderer for Godot 4.7 (C#) with Claude Code, starting from a single design doc"

2. help — A post where someone is stuck, confused, or trying to confirm whether a problem is on their end or Claude's.
   Example 1: "This week's Claude Reset - Anyone else noticing unusually high usage all of a sudden?"
   Example 2: "Anyone else's Claude suddenly started using 'read-only' for anything that could involve a write?"

3. discussion — A post sharing a take, reaction, tip, news, or opinion about Claude or Anthropic without showing a build or asking for help.
   Example 1: "Anthropic told the IMF that it expects Claude to lead 80-90% of frontier model training by next year"
   Example 2: "3 small Claude Code habits from other people's setups that I liked"

Boundary rule: If the post includes a working output like a demo, a screenshot of the thing running, or a playable link, it is showcase. If it describes a method or opinion without showing a finished result, it is discussion, even if the author built something to arrive at that opinion.

## Milestone 4: Hard cases

1. "Can we please start building apps and systems to save humanity." I went back and forth between showcase and discussion. The author actually lists four things they built, so there is working output. But the whole point of the post is telling the community to stop making clones. The projects are just backup for the argument. I went discussion because the post is trying to start a conversation, not show off a build.

2. "Claude and Codex debate #3." This one was tricky because the author released a new version of Hellhound with a real feature (text to speech). But the post itself is framed around a philosophy question about AI consciousness. The tool is just the vehicle. I went discussion because the debate is what the post is about, not the app update.

3. "Opus 5.5 Taking so long and token usage." The author says they are not complaining and frames it like sharing an observation. But they also straight up ask if anyone found workarounds. That is asking for a fix. I went help because the real reason they posted is to solve a problem, even if the tone is casual.
